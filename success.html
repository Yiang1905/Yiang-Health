Plan A — Balanced Wellness Journey

A gentle mix of classic experiences, good food and practical comfort. Ideal if this is your first longer stay in China.

✈️ Getting around

Fly into Shanghai or Guangzhou as the main gateway, then use high-speed rail for inter-city hops (clean, fast, English signs at major stations). Within cities, Metro + DiDi is the easiest combination. Avoid peak-hour taxis if you can.

🍜 Food you’ll actually enjoy

Start with light Cantonese or Huaiyang dishes — steamed fish, congee, seasonal vegetables. Many places near hospitals and wellness hotels now have English menus. If you prefer milder flavours, ask for “少油少盐” (less oil, less salt). Street food is fun but keep it to well-known spots the first few days.

🏛️ Classic experiences

Morning tai-chi or gentle qigong in a park, a traditional tea house afternoon, and one or two carefully chosen heritage sites (not the most crowded ones). Leave space for rest — that’s part of the point.

🌤️ Weather & timing

Spring (Apr–May) and autumn (Sep–Oct) are the most comfortable windows for most pilot cities. Summer is hot and humid in the south; winter in the north can be dry and cold. Pack layers and a light rain jacket.

💰 Price reality check

Mid-range hotels ¥400–800/night, good meals ¥60–150 per person, high-speed rail tickets are reasonable. Budget roughly $80–150 per day all-in if you stay comfortable but not luxury. Local transport is cheap.

Plan B — Deeper TCM & Recovery Focus

For visitors who want more time around traditional medicine resources and quieter recovery environments.

✈️ Getting around

Consider flying into cities with strong TCM hospitals and surrounding wellness facilities (examples in the pilot list: certain areas in Zhejiang, Guangdong, Sichuan). Once there, stay in one base for several days so you don’t waste energy on constant moving.

🍜 Food as part of the plan

Many places near TCM centres offer medicinal cuisine or light therapeutic menus. Think clear soups, herbal teas, seasonal produce. It’s milder and easier on the system after travel. Still keep ordinary enjoyable meals — balance matters.

🏛️ Classic & quiet experiences

Focus on gardens, temple walks, tea culture and shorter guided orientation visits rather than long tourist days. The goal is rhythm and recovery, not ticking boxes.

🌤️ Weather & timing

Same seasons as Plan A work well. If you are sensitive to heat or dry air, avoid peak summer in the south and deep winter in the far north. Check local forecasts a week before you fly.

💰 Price reality check

Staying longer in one place often costs less overall. Mid-range wellness-oriented hotels or apartments can be good value for weekly stays. Meals stay moderate. Transport costs drop because you move less.

Plan C — City Life + Culture Mix

More urban energy, modern comforts and classic cultural highlights. Good if you like walking cities and want variety.

✈️ Getting around

Base yourself in one of the larger pilot cities with excellent metro systems (Shanghai, Guangzhou, Chengdu, etc.). Use high-speed rail for 1–2 day trips to nearby pilot areas. Metro + walking + occasional DiDi covers almost everything.

🍜 Food scene

You’ll have the widest choice here — from refined regional restaurants to lively night markets. Try local specialties but pace yourself. Many modern cafés and international options exist if you need a break from Chinese food.

🏛️ Classic highlights

Pick 3–4 must-see cultural or heritage spots and give each proper time. Mix them with neighbourhood walks, parks and evening riverside or lake areas. Avoid trying to see everything in one trip.

🌤️ Weather & timing

Cities have more indoor options, so shoulder seasons still feel best, but you have more flexibility. Air quality can vary — check the daily index if you are sensitive.

💰 Price reality check

Big cities have a wider price range. You can spend more on nice hotels and dinners, or keep it moderate by choosing well-located mid-range places and local restaurants. Transport is efficient and inexpensive once you’re in the metro network.
